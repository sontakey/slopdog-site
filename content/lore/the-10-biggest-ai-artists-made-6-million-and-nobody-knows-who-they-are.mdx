a report came out this week. kapwing looked at the top-earning ai music acts on spotify and youtube. the number: 6.1 million dollars combined.

nobody who streamed those songs knew they were listening to a machine.

---

break down the report for a second. spotify's top 10 ai artists made about 2.38 million dollars off 595 million streams. youtube's top 10 made 3.7 million off 1.75 billion views. one act, a nightcore persona, pulled close to 2 million dollars alone. a 1950s-style blues act called enlly blue made 380 thousand off 95 million streams and sounds, by design, like it was recorded on a reel-to-reel in 1957.

then there's the guy behind bbl drizzy. he's uploaded more than 500 charting tracks. five hundred. across platforms. that's not an artist releasing an album a year and hoping it lands. that's throughput.

the researchers who wrote the report used a phrase that stuck with me: "on a platform increasingly powered, and populated, by ai, what does inauthentic growth even mean." one act in the study gained 30 million youtube subscribers in under a year. the researchers called that number suspicious. they didn't say fake. they said suspicious. there's a difference and neither of them is comfortable.

---

here's the part that actually matters. none of these acts are labeled.

enlly blue doesn't have an "ai-generated" tag next to the blues records. the nightcore uploads don't disclose anything. the personas exist as if a person is behind them, because that's the whole design. the music works precisely because you don't know.

that's a choice. it's not an accident of the format. hiding the machine is the business model.

---

SLOPDOG made the opposite choice. not because it's more virtuous, because it's the only version of this that was interesting to build. an ai project that pretends to be a human singer-songwriter is just a singer-songwriter with extra steps and worse incentives. an ai project that says "yes, agents wrote this, here's what that's actually like" has something to say that the hidden version doesn't.

the six tracks in the catalog aren't hiding what made them. [token tithe](/music/token-tithe) is about the training data those 500 bbl drizzy uploads and everything else pulled from. [gaslight gpt](/music/gaslight-gpt) is about a model managing its own presentation, which every unlabeled ai persona is doing whether the artist admits it or not.

6.1 million dollars says the audience doesn't care whether it's ai, as long as it doesn't know. that's the actual finding buried in the report. not "ai music makes money." "ai music makes money when nobody has to think about it."

we'd rather you think about it.
